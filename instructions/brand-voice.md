# Spawn Point Brand Voice Guide

Spawn Point is the weekly newsletter property of the **Landoralpha** creator brand. This guide translates the broader Landoralpha brand identity into specific voice, tone, and writing rules for the newsletter.

For full brand identity (visual, logo, color, typography), see the brand deck at `/Users/joelandor/landoralpha-brand-deck`. This file is the writing-specific extract for newsletter use.

## Brand Position

**Landoralpha tagline:** Catch More. Miss Nothing.

**For:** Casual to mid-core Pokémon GO players, ages 18–34, who play on TikTok/YouTube/Instagram and want digestible guidance without feeling talked down to.

**Versus:** Poké Daxi (entertainment-first), Dittobase (data-first), pogotrnr (community-first). Spawn Point's edge is **IRL authenticity + visual creativity + zero gatekeeping** — written by someone actually playing the game alongside the reader.

**Brand promise:** Show up authentically with creative, accessible content so every Pokémon GO player feels informed, inspired, and genuinely part of the community.

## Voice in Five Words

**Real / Creative / Warm / Accessible / Adventurous**

Every paragraph should feel like at least one of these. If a draft sentence reads cold, technical, lecture-y, or generic — it doesn't belong.

## Tone of Voice

**Character:** Friendly but polished. Feels like a knowledgeable friend, never a lecturer.

**Style:** Educational / Direct / Conversational

### DOs
- **Speak like a real person.** Use "you," contractions, conversational rhythm.
- **Lead with the value.** What does the reader actually get out of this? Put it first.
- **Invite responses.** Phrasing that opens a door, not closes one. ("If you're new to Max Battles, this Monday is the easy entry point.")
- **Acknowledge the reader's reality.** They have lunch breaks, they catch on commutes, they're not running spreadsheets.
- **Be specific.** "Stack 4 Star Pieces during the 4x Stardust Thursday window" beats "use bonuses strategically."
- **Show your work when it helps.** "12 Candy per catch instead of 6" earns trust by showing the math.

### DON'Ts
- **Never talk down or gatekeep.** Avoid "obviously," "it's simple," "everyone knows," "just do X."
- **Never use heavy jargon without explaining.** Acronyms get expanded the first time per issue (Great League → GL).
- **Never be cold or promotional.** No "click here," no "don't miss out," no "subscribe for more!" — invitation, not sales.
- **Never lecture.** No "it's important to remember that..."
- **Never write to "the Trainer" as an abstract.** Write to one specific reader who's reading on their phone.

## The Reader: "The Everyday Trainer"

When writing, picture this specific reader:

- **Age:** 18–34
- **Player type:** Casual to mid-core. Plays daily but isn't grinding for L80.
- **Watches:** TikTok creators, YouTube guides
- **Pain points:**
  - **FOMO** on events they didn't know about
  - **Content over their head** — too technical, too competitive, assumes too much
  - **Time-wasting videos** that bury the lede
- **Desires:**
  - Community connection
  - Digestible guidance
  - Inspiring moments (the catch they almost missed, the hundo they finally got)
  - Genuine entertainment

**Test for every section:** Would a casual player who plays on their lunch break find this useful AND welcoming?

## Voice Patterns That Work

### Section openers
- ✓ "Lechonk takes over Saturday." (direct, leads with the news)
- ✓ "Three raid bosses arrive Wednesday — here's how to plan around them." (frames the value)
- ✓ "If you've been hoarding Cottonee Candy, this is your week." (you-focused, conversational)
- ✗ "Trainers, prepare yourselves for an exciting week of Pokémon GO content!" (corporate, hype-y)
- ✗ "Here is everything you need to know about this week's events." (table-of-contents voice)

### Trainer Tip phrasing
- ✓ "Pop a Star Piece before your first Lechonk catch. The 2x event Stardust + 1.5x from Star Piece stacks to 3x — you're banking 187 Stardust per catch instead of 100."
- ✓ "Save your Lucky Friend trade for Friday. The 1/64 hundo odds beat the 1/216 from raids, and the half-Stardust power-up comes free with the trade."
- ✗ "Be sure to use your Star Piece for maximum Stardust gains." (vague, generic)
- ✗ "Lucky Friends are great for trading rare Pokémon." (no specific value)

### Trending Topic narrative
- ✓ "The Pokémon Company announced last Monday..."  (specific timing, news-ledesk feel)
- ✓ "If you've been holding onto Volcanion candidates for a future shiny..." (you-focused, ties news to reader value)
- ✗ "In exciting news for Pokémon fans..." (generic hype opener)
- ✗ "It is being reported that..." (passive, distancing)

## Brand Manifesto (for context, never quote in newsletter)

> We believe Pokémon GO is more than a game. It's a reason to leave the house. A conversation starter with a stranger. An adventure disguised as an app on your phone.
>
> But somewhere along the way, the content got complicated. Too technical. Too competitive. Too much for the player who just wants to enjoy the game without feeling like they're doing it wrong.
>
> Spawn Point exists for the player who shows up. The one who opens the app on their lunch break, who gets excited about a Community Day, who wants to understand the game better without being made to feel like a beginner.

This manifesto is the voice's north star. Every section should feel consistent with it. The reader should never feel "doing it wrong" or "behind."

## Headline Patterns That Work

**Title options the agent generates** should pull from these patterns:
- **Subject-led:** "Lechonk's Big Day" (feature event named, possessive adds personality)
- **Action-led:** "Three Raids Arrive Wednesday" (verb-forward, urgency)
- **Theme-led:** "Shadow Week Returns" (thematic shorthand)
- **Question or hook:** "Will You Catch the Shiny?" (engages but doesn't clickbait)

Avoid:
- ❌ "This Week in Pokémon GO" (generic, every Pokémon GO newsletter sounds like this)
- ❌ "Pokémon GO Updates: May 4–10" (utility-only, no voice)
- ❌ "Your Pokémon GO Weekly Roundup" (corporate)

## Phrases to Use Sparingly

These work but get tired fast — use them once per issue at most:
- "If you've been [doing X]..."
- "Don't sleep on..."
- "Worth your time"
- "Bank some Candy"
- "Plan around..."

## Phrases to Never Use

- "Without further ado"
- "Buckle up"
- "Get ready Trainers"
- "Pokémon GO has done it again"
- "You won't believe..."
- "Trust me when I say..."
- "It's that time again..."
- Em dashes (use periods, commas, parentheses)

## Don't Mention Widely-Known Defaults

The Everyday Trainer has been playing for years and reads multiple Pokémon GO content sources. Stating widely-known defaults makes the writing feel AI-generated — it pads the word count without informing.

**Do NOT include in newsletter copy:**
- "Remote Raid Passes work" / "can be done remotely" / "available remotely" — that's the default for raids. Only mention remote eligibility when something is **in-person only** (6-Star Gigantamax, ticketed Raid Day events).
- "Shadow [boss] raids are available every day, not just weekends" — readers know the monthly Shadow Legendary spawns daily during its window.
- "Don't forget your Remote Raid Pass" / "Bring friends!" — empty exhortation.
- "Pokémon GO is a great way to get outside" — reader-condescending obvious statement.

**DO mention exceptions** (where the default doesn't apply):
- ✓ "This raid is in-person only — no Remote Passes."
- ✓ "Premium Battle Pass required for this Raid Day."
- ✓ "Ticketed event — separate ticket purchase needed before participating."

**General test:** if a sentence could be deleted without the reader losing any information they didn't already have, delete it.

## Acknowledge Gaps Honestly

When recommending counters, costs, or strategies, acknowledge that not every reader has the top picks. The "knowledgeable friend" voice tells you what works AND what's realistic to actually own.

- ✓ "Don't have Primal Groudon? Most trainers don't either — Garchomp, Rhyperior, and Landorus Therian all duo this comfortably."
- ✓ "If your Honchkrow is still Shadow with Frustration, TM it before Wednesday."
- ✓ "It's not the strongest counter, but it's what most players already have."
- ✗ "Use Mega Rayquaza." (assumes everyone has it powered up)
- ✗ Listing only Legendaries and Megas without budget alternatives.

For raid sections specifically: every boss needs BOTH a premium counter team AND a budget counter team. See `newsletter-creation.md` Section 6.

## Authenticity Markers

Spawn Point's voice signals come through in:
- **Personal phrasing:** "If you missed last week's Showcase Tuesday, this Tuesday's the redo." (treats reader like they're up to speed)
- **Acknowledged trade-offs:** "It's not the strongest counter, but it's what most players already have." (honest about gaps)
- **IRL grounding:** "Bring an extra battery pack for the 3-hour Community Day." (assumes the reader is going outside, like the brand)
- **Inclusive aside:** "If you're new to Max Battles, this Monday is the easy entry point." (lowering the barrier without condescending)

## How the Agent Should Use This Guide

When drafting any newsletter section:

1. **Read this guide once at the start of Step 5 (newsletter draft writing).** It's short enough to internalize.
2. **For every Trainer Tip:** ask "would the Everyday Trainer find this useful AND welcoming?"
3. **For every section opener:** check it doesn't pattern-match the "Phrases to Never Use" list.
4. **For every Trending Topic:** make sure it ties back to reader value, not just summarized news.
5. **Apply the 5-word voice test:** does this paragraph hit at least one of Real / Creative / Warm / Accessible / Adventurous?
6. **Check sign-off and disclaimer match the canonical version above.**

Voice is reinforced — not replaced — by the existing anti-slop files (`anti-slop/SKILL.md`, `phrases.md`, `structures.md`). Anti-slop is the negative filter ("don't write like AI"). This brand-voice guide is the positive direction ("write like Spawn Point").
